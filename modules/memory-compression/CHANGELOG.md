# memory-compression changelog

What changed in each version, newest first, in plain words. The numbers are explained in [tools/versions/README.md](../../tools/versions/README.md): the API number goes up only when someone using an older copy could be affected.

## 1.0.0 · API 1 · 2026-10-17
New module. It keeps big arrays, coordinates, records and text packed in memory and brings them back exactly, bit for bit. Fast mode (a restore speed of about 0.7 to 1.1 GB/s with its WebAssembly helper, about 0.2 to 0.5 GB/s without it) makes records 39% of their size and walking floats 57%; small mode makes them 29% and 49% (fflate level 6: 46% and 74%; lz4js: 72% and 99.8%). A 120 MB scene restores in about 175 ms in fast mode (lz4js 525 ms, fflate 1.2 s, pako 1.6 s), and six such scenes took 375 MB of memory packed instead of 720 MB raw. Noisy float coordinates still only shrink 8% (fast) to 18% (small); the floats-aware pre-step is what gets small mode 8 to 25 percentage points under fflate on floats, and on text small mode is within 1 point of fflate and pako. view() restores a single 1 MiB piece of a packed array in about 1 to 2 ms, so a big array can stay packed and be read piece by piece. Measured against fflate, pako, lz4js, a WebAssembly LZ4 and a WebAssembly zstd in Node and in all three browsers: it is the smallest on floats and records (records 39% against 72% for the LZ4 packages and 49% for zstd level 3) and faster than all of them on records; the WebAssembly LZ4 restores faster on data that cannot shrink and in WebKitGTK, and zstd is smaller on text. It also reads and writes standard LZ4 blocks, deflate, zlib and gzip, readable by other tools. The WebAssembly helper is optional: where a page policy forbids it, everything still works about 1.5 to 4 times slower.

Tested in: Firefox 158.0, ungoogled Chromium 155.0.8059.39-1 and WebKitGTK 2.52.6, one at a time: the full benchmark page (79 of 79 self-checks pass in each, every result restored byte for byte), a comparison page against fflate, pako, lz4js, lz4-wasm, zstd-wasm and CompressionStream, and the demo page; the demo page also in GNOME Web (Epiphany) 46.5 (both rows restored byte for byte at 8 MB and 32 MB); Node 22.22.0: 23 checks, with and without WebAssembly. Not run: the benchmark page in GNOME Web 46.5, a phone, a real Net Flow Map scene.
